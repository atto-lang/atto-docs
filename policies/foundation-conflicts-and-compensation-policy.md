# Atto Foundation — Conflict-of-Interest and Compensation Approval Policy

**Version:** 0.1 | **Status:** PROPOSED BOARD POLICY — NOT ADOPTED | **Owner:** [actual legal Foundation entity]  
**Purpose:** Preserve charitable independence while permitting reasonable pay for real services and arm's-length transactions involving IP. This is a governance proposal, **not** a promise of salary, royalties, a 4% economic stake or membership votes.

> **Adoption gate:** This policy is effective only if and when the Foundation is legally formed, a duly empowered disinterested board approves it, and the adoption record below is completed. Confirm the Foundation's legal status and any applicable state-law requirements with nonprofit counsel. No IRS recognition is presumed.

## 1. Governing principles

The Foundation is intended to qualify as a charitable organization under IRC 501(c)(3). No part of its net earnings may inure to a private shareholder or individual. Fair value payments for legitimate work or property that further exempt purposes are different from private distributions. A founder's or engineer's title does not itself entitle them to salary, royalties, deferred compensation, 4% of receipts, grants or donated funds, ownership or a board seat.

This policy applies to directors, officers, key personnel, founding contributors, their family members, related for-profit entities (including AttoPL Co / Atto Co), and parties in which these persons have material interests. Review all related-party transactions, including employment, consulting, research grants, IP purchases/licenses, equipment, shared staff, cloud services and reimbursements.

## 2. Annual disclosures and event-based conflicts

Each director, officer and covered insider must provide an initial and at least annual written disclosure of financial interests, relationships with AttoPL Co, consulting agreements, related-party employment, IP ownership, family relationships, and potential competing loyalties. Disclose a new conflict **before** discussions of the relevant transaction. Record the disclosure in a restricted register. Disclosures alone do not automatically prohibit lawful deals; they trigger independent review.

An "interested person" is one with an actual or potential financial or decision-making conflict; the Foundation must additionally assess whether a person is a "disqualified person" for IRC 4958. Do not infer that the tax classification depends solely on a 4% holding in another entity. Counsel determines the applicable rules.

## 3. Independent approval and recusal

Before a material conflicted arrangement is entered into or material compensation is increased:

1. The interested person discloses all relevant facts and may answer factual questions but **leaves during deliberation and voting** and cannot control or veto the outcome.
2. A quorum of appropriately empowered **disinterested** directors or committee members determines whether the deal advances the charitable mission and is reasonable. If no eligible decision-makers remain, defer the transaction, obtain outside counsel and lawful board reconstitution/independent review as needed; **do not use an interested person's signature as substitute approval**.
3. The authorized body evaluates alternatives, total compensation across Foundation and related entities, proposed scope, market comparables, donor restrictions, budget sufficiency, and non-duplication of prior payments.
4. Document affirmative findings, the votes and abstentions, any dissent, and why the consideration is no greater than fair value and serves charitable objectives.
5. The Foundation executes the specific work order or IP license **only after** the independent approval, by an authorized unconflicted representative.

No Founder or Contributor may set their own pay, choose an IP royalty rate unilaterally, approve their own purchase or direct Foundation resources to themselves.

## 4. Compensation and fair-market-value review

Use appropriately relevant external data (similarly situated charitable and taxable employers, regional salary surveys, independent written offers, pricing quotes or an independent professional appraisal). Document what was compared, when it was obtained, the duties/services or IP rights compared, and any adjustments. Consider **all** forms of compensation, including salaries, bonus, fringe benefits, contractor fees, payments by controlled/related organizations and royalties when relevant.

The Foundation must not approve an uncapped fixed percentage of organization-wide donations, contributions, income, unrestricted receipts or surplus as "founding residuals." A genuine license for independently owned IP may use a properly valued, separately specified transaction-based royalty base, with a written limitation, records, audit/termination terms and review by counsel. Donations and restricted grants must **never** be used as the royalty base.

Apply the IRS rebuttable-presumption process when relevant: advance approval by unconflicted authorized persons; appropriate comparability data; and contemporaneous documentation of the basis, names, deliberation, votes and recusals. Under the federal rule, prepare records before the later of the next meeting or 60 days after approval, and have them approved as accurate within a reasonable time. This is **an approval-control practice, not a legal safe harbor guarantee**. Counsel must separately check 4958, state nonprofit law and charity-law restrictions.

## 5. Records and periodic oversight

Keep approval minutes, market-data evidence, restricted-person disclosures, signed engagements/licenses, invoices, deliverable acceptance, disbursement receipts, IP title evidence, annual reviews, and appropriate tax filings in restricted storage. Do not commit personal information, signed agreements, medical or payroll records, account numbers or trade secrets to the shared `atto-docs` repo.

Each year the board shall review related-party transactions and this policy for mission alignment, organizational independence, reasonable compensation and adequate reporting (including IRS Form 990 / Schedule L when applicable).

All commitments are subject to applicable wage, tax, immigration, worker classification and reimbursement law. Never reclassify employees as unpaid volunteers to avoid required wages. Honor payment for legally earned work.
