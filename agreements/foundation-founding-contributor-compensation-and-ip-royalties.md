## ARTICLE 1 — CONTRIBUTOR STATUS; NO FOUNDATION OWNERSHIP

1. The Foundation may recognize a Contributor's founding status and continued substantive contributions to its legitimate charitable, scientific, educational, or public-benefit work. This status conveys **no private equity, no percentage ownership, no dividend entitlement, no beneficial interest in Foundation assets, and no right to Foundation earnings or donations**.
2. The Contributor has **no ordinary voting rights**, no board or trustee seat, no officer or manager appointment power, and no authority to direct grants, budgets, executive compensation, hiring, or charitable expenditures by virtue of founding status. Nothing waives rights that cannot legally be waived.
3. This agreement grants **no automatic salary** and **no royalty rate**; it does not grant a job, guaranteed hours, guaranteed engagements, or a unilateral right to elect payment. It sets enforceable terms *once an authorized written engagement or IP license is separately approved and signed*, and grants the proposal-and-review process stated in Article 2.
4. A 4% non-voting interest in AttoPL Co / Atto Co, if separately issued, remains a Company equity matter and does **not** become Foundation ownership or revenue participation.

## ARTICLE 2 — RIGHT TO REQUEST PAID ENGAGEMENTS; INDEPENDENT REVIEW

1. The Contributor may submit a written proposal to perform compensable Foundation services (for example, language/compiler research, specification work, security reviews, infrastructure maintenance, educational programs, public documentation, or permitted licensing work). The Foundation shall provide a response within **30 calendar days**, where practicable, identifying whether the proposal is approved, requires more information, or is declined, together with a brief explanation.
2. The Foundation's authorized **disinterested** decision-makers must approve scope, compensation, terms and funding **in advance**, after considering relevant independent market comparables, total compensation, potential conflicts, charitable purpose, and available unrestricted funds. The Contributor may not participate in a vote or decision on their own compensation.
3. Approval is recorded in **Schedule A — Paid Services Work Order** or a separate written employment agreement, signed by an authorized Foundation representative **before compensable services start**. Oral promises, GitHub issue assignments, merged PRs, this status agreement, or the Contributor's election to take a salary are not, by themselves, funded payment obligations.
4. **Once a valid paid work order or employment agreement is authorized and signed, accepted compensable services create an enforceable contractual right to the agreed payment** on the documented payment schedule, subject to applicable law. The Foundation must not unilaterally erase payment already earned by terminating an assignment.
5. A contributor may choose to **decline** a proposed paid engagement and continue permitted independent contributions. The Foundation may decline a proposed engagement for good-faith funding, program, compliance, or charitable-purpose reasons; founding status does not reserve a salaried position.
6. **Wage and classification laws take priority.** The Foundation must not label work "volunteer" when applicable law requires compensation, nor use promised future pay to avoid timely wages or required benefits.

## ARTICLE 3 — PERMISSIBLE FORMS OF COMPENSATION

### 3.1 Salary and wages

The Foundation may employ a Contributor under a separately signed employment contract establishing role, compensation, hours or employment classification, payroll, taxes, benefits, and applicable worker protections. Salary is payment for services, **not** a distribution on an ownership stake.

### 3.2 Approved contractor or project fees

A pre-approved written work order may set reasonable hourly fees, fixed milestones, research stipends for services, or project-based payments. The Foundation must document services actually rendered and acceptance criteria.

### 3.3 Reasonable bonuses

Reasonable, independently approved performance bonuses may be offered for objectively described services or milestones, taking **aggregate** compensation and comparable arrangements into account. No bonus based solely on founder title, unrestricted Foundation income, gifts, grant balances, or net earnings is granted.

### 3.4 Deferred compensation — only through separate compliant plan

The parties shall **not** automatically accumulate unpaid salary, deferred compensation, vested service credits, a perpetual payment account, or a payment claim for volunteer work under this Agreement. Any future deferral must be established **prospectively** through legally reviewed documents addressing applicable employment, tax, IRC 457/409A where relevant, state-law and funding constraints. Legally required wages cannot be waived or deferred unlawfully.

## ARTICLE 4 — IP LICENSE ROYALTIES; NO AUTOMATIC RESIDUALS

1. A Contributor may propose licensing **specifically identified, independently owned or lawfully retained pre-existing IP** to the Foundation if the Foundation has a genuine mission-related need for it.
2. **No royalty exists unless a separate, prior-written, independently approved IP license is signed** identifying: the exact IP and chain of title; permitted uses, territory, term and sublicensing; the demonstrated fair-market value; an appropriately documented fixed fee, per-use rate, or other commercially reasonable compensation formula; payment dates, reporting, audit terms, termination, and any related-party disclosure.
3. A reasonable, arm's-length percentage of **clearly defined consideration from a genuine IP transaction** may be proposed only if counsel determines it does not create impermissible private inurement or excessive private benefit and its economics are supported by comparables. **There is no default 4% royalty** and no entitlement measured by Foundation-wide donations, grants, receipts, net income, earnings, charitable assets, or unrelated product income.
4. **No double payment:** An inventor or author cannot claim royalties merely for work already assigned to or paid for by the Foundation or AttoPL Co, absent a clear separately enforceable retained right and fair-value exchange. A Contributor must not represent Foundation-owned or third-party code as their personal royalty-bearing asset.
5. Royalties under a valid license, if any, may continue after a Contributor stops working **only for the license's independently negotiated and enforceable term**, subject to its termination and applicable law, not because of founding status.
6. The Foundation must not disguise compensation for services or insider distributions as "royalties" or "residuals." Any cross-entity IP license with AttoPL Co must also have separate approvals and an arm's-length business purpose.

## ARTICLE 5 — CONTRIBUTION RECOGNITION; NO EQUITY VESTING

1. For consistency with the companion AttoPL Co contributor plan, qualifying work may be recorded as **three (3) accepted substantive contributions per month**, with a **three-month initial qualification period**. Ongoing recognition may be tracked for **36 additional qualifying monthly periods**, following the commercial plan's earliest **39-month** completion model.
2. **These periods do not vest Foundation shares, charity assets, residual percentages, future salaries, or deferred compensation.** Recognition alone does not authorize payment for otherwise unpaid work.
3. Qualification and continued status may support the Foundation's good-faith review of future compensated engagements, but every fee, salary, or royalty still requires a separately approved lawful arrangement. Existing payment obligations from an executed work order or license survive according to their terms.
4. Contributions are evaluated on substantive technical or program value, security, test evidence, and charitable purpose, not artificial commit counts. The Foundation may approve reasonable exceptions without converting status into ownership.

## ARTICLE 6 — GOVERNANCE; CONFLICTS; DOCUMENTATION

1. The Foundation's independent board or other lawfully authorized disinterested body retains full fiduciary decision-making authority. No Contributor may set their own salary, royalty or approved program budget, participate in a decision in which they have a disqualifying conflict, or veto disinterested Foundation decisions.
2. Decisions on compensation and IP transactions must be approved in advance by authorized unconflicted persons, based on appropriate comparability information and documented contemporaneously (including the proposed transaction, participants, recusals, comparable evidence, and decision).
3. All wages, cash fees, bonuses, fringe benefits, royalties, payments from related entities where relevant, and potential insider relationships must be examined for reasonableness and IRS section 4958 exposure. Applicable Form 990/Schedule L disclosures and Foundation policies must be followed.
4. **Charitable donations, restricted grants, donor-advised funds, and Foundation net earnings cannot be diverted as founder distributions.** Legitimate program costs may be funded only under their valid restrictions and proper charitable purpose.
5. Nothing promises that the Foundation is now IRS-recognized, has raised funds, has hired staff, or has passed legal due diligence.

## ARTICLE 7 — IP, PROPRIETARY SOURCE AND SECURITY

1. AttoLang is currently **proprietary, not open source**. Access to any confidential repository does not grant rights to publish, distribute, sublicense or release that code. The Founding Engineer private-fork/name restrictions, if separately granted, remain independent.
2. Work product, assigned inventions, pre-existing exclusions, confidentiality, AI-tool disclosures, and third-party licenses are governed by written project and IP agreements. Any licensed IP must be listed in **Schedule B** before royalty promises are made.
3. No Contributor may provide confidential Foundation or AttoPL Co material to third-party AI tools without authorization, or use Foundation marks or the "Atto"/"AttoLang" names for an independent fork without permission.

## ARTICLE 8 — TERMINATION AND RIGHTS THAT SURVIVE

1. Either party may end the unpaid founding-status relationship on written notice, subject to law, confidentiality and any agreed transition duties. The termination of a signed employment contract or paid work order is determined by that instrument and mandatory law.
2. The Foundation must pay **earned and legally owed** wages or approved work-order fees, and honor lawful royalties under a separate license on its terms. No perpetual Foundation economic entitlement survives merely by virtue of founding status.
3. Unapproved applications for work, unvested Company equity, and potential future projects do not become payable obligations of the Foundation. Company equity continues according to its own documents.

## ARTICLE 9 — LAW, EXECUTION AND INDEPENDENT AGREEMENTS

1. Governing law: **[jurisdiction — to be completed by counsel]**. Mandatory employment, charity, corporate, tax, intellectual-property, and donor-restriction laws govern notwithstanding contrary language.
2. No private rights against the Foundation arise until the correct Foundation entity is legally formed, authorized, and executes an appropriate instrument. This draft is not an offer of unpaid wages or guaranteed compensation.
3. Amendments and waivers of material rights must be signed by authorized parties. A separate funded work order or IP license is required before payment arises, except when mandatory law already creates a payment obligation.
4. The intended four-percent Company non-voting equity award remains independent. This document **does not replace it**.
